An explorer for one question: when a video shows something physically impossible — an object passing through a wall, or vanishing behind one and never coming back — what does V-JEPA 2's internal world model do? We read that out two ways and let you watch it on the actual frames: the model's “surprise” (how wrong its prediction of the next moment is), and its latent surface (how the representation itself moves, splits, or collapses through the clip).

How to read the Explore tab

Pick a scene on the left: a matched possible / impossible pair plays in lockstep, with the model's reaction drawn underneath and a playhead riding it. A run is shown one of two ways:

  • Surprise readout — two curves trace prediction error frame by frame; a per-patch surprise heatmap (toggle) shows what the model finds unexpected, with a faint pixel-diff band as an honesty check (what's visually different, not necessarily where physics breaks).
  • Latent surface — a shared-PCA map of both clips' representation trajectories (watch them share a path, then split), plus three rails: effective rank (collapse), latent velocity vs pixel flow (is a change motion or reorganization?), and possible−impossible divergence against a within-scene null. Toggle the localized divergence heatmap to see where on the frame the two representations differ.

A latent-surface run adds a view switcher over its per-clip lenses: Latent surface (above), Anticipation (the predictor's guessed future vs what actually happened, the de-biased gap being where it mispredicted), and Dense features (one clip-averaged map of how the model partitions the scene).

The separate Population tab holds the two read-outs that are the same for every clip (they summarize the whole set, so they don't belong per-pair): Probes — can a simple classifier read a fact (“is this impossible?”, “which scene type?”, “how much motion?”) off the frozen features, vs a shuffle null, and at what depth it emerges — and the Violation map — every clip's impossible-event “nudge” as a map + similarity grid, with a clip preview so a cluster leads back to the footage.

The established result — surprise is a motion detector here

Boil each clip to a single surprise number and ask “was the impossible clip more surprising than its possible twin?” and the answer is basically a coin flip: across 180 matched pairs the accuracy sits near chance (0.50). Controls (NULL_CONTROLS.md) show the surprise tracks how much is moving (r = 0.855 with motion energy) far more than whether physics broke. This is a fact about the checkpoint, not the method: our exact scoring reproduces the authors' ~92% on their published numbers, and the same architecture with V-JEPA 1's how-to weights scores ~92% on this benchmark through our pipeline. So V-JEPA 2 ViT-L is the weak model for this task — not the approach or world-models in general. Detail in CROSS_CHECK.md and RECONCILIATION.md.

What we're exploring now — the latent surface

The surprise scalar threw away the representation it was computed from — and that's where the physics signal got laundered into motion. The latent surface goes back up to that representation to ask a sharper, identifiability question: do the latent world-variables move, split, or collapse around a violation? This is exploratory, not a result yet. On vitl.pt so far: effective rank sits ~30 of 1024, and latent velocity barely correlates with motion (r ≈ 0.18) — interesting, but the divergence nulls we've tried still mix in scene/scenario variation, so nothing here is a verdict. The honest next test is the localized heatmap (does the difference land on the violation or smear over motion?) and the same surface on the strong how-to checkpoint. Method, caveats, and open questions: LATENT_SURFACE.md.
